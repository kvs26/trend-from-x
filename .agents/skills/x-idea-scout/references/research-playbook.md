# Research playbook

## Find conversations that can lead to a product

Use the current request to judge relevance. Otherwise include both useful tools and playful experiences without a product-direction question. First obtain the account-category selection described in `SKILL.md`, then complete the selected-account pass below before producing the full findings report. Broad searches and deeper lead exploration supplement that pass; they do not replace it.

## Complete the watchlist pass

Read [the account watchlist](x-account-watchlist.md), calculate the per-category counts for the opening multi-selection menu, and include each category's plain-language meaning alongside its name and count. Use the user's selection to form the required account set. Include all accounts only when the user chooses all categories. Deduplicate the selected set, preserve watchlist order, and record the selected categories and total count. Finish each selected account's initial inspection before moving to the next. Search each account individually; a combined `from:` search or a few famous accounts does not establish coverage of the selected set.

For each account:

1. Confirm the profile belongs to the intended person. If the handle has changed, verify the replacement using the person's own site or a clear profile identity signal; preserve the original and resolved handle in the run's record.
2. Inspect **at least one month of previous posts through the run date** for problems, requests, workarounds, launches, demonstrations, results, and useful discussions. Calculate and record the start and end dates in the user's timezone; use a longer requested range when provided. Cover the whole window rather than stopping at the first screen or a few recent posts. Check dates on pinned posts rather than treating them as recent. Use account-specific searches and date slices when needed to reach the older portion of a high-volume account's month; a filtered or top-results search alone does not establish full-window inspection. Choose additional search terms from observed context rather than running a fixed keyword grid.
3. Capture every distinct relevant signal surfaced by the inspection, with its direct link, date, and a short evidence note. For a relevant post that references another post, open the referenced original, expand the relevant thread, and follow further references when they materially affect the claim, problem, workflow, or result. This includes older posts and accounts outside the watchlist. Preserve the source chain and distinguish historical context from recent evidence. Resolve essential reference context during the account inspection rather than deferring it to a user follow-up. Stop a chain when its original evidence and relevant context are clear, it repeats, or further access is unavailable; disclose material unresolved references. Do not force an idea out of unrelated posts or interpret an empty search as proof that the account has no relevant posts.
4. Record one outcome: **checked — findings**, **checked — nothing relevant found**, **coverage incomplete**, **access limited**, or **unavailable**. Record **not yet checked** for unfinished entries. For a completed empty inspection, tell the user "Nothing relevant found" and give the inspected window. If the full month could not be inspected, record the actual dates and gap rather than using the empty-result label. Note whether the evidence came from the live timeline, account-specific search, original website, or search snippets. Snippets alone count as limited coverage.

Keep a coverage table for the selected accounts with account, required date range, actually inspected date range, access/source type, outcome, finding links or IDs, and any limitation. Preserve it in the run's research report with the next account if interrupted. Attempt every selected account even when some profiles are blocked. If an access problem applies to the whole source, continue with available account-specific public evidence and identify the affected accounts; do not simulate successful checks or claim full live-X coverage. Summarize totals against the selected account count, separating accounts attempted, accounts inspected across the full monthly window, and incomplete or access-limited accounts. Unselected categories are outside scope and are not coverage failures.

Resolve the source references needed to understand each selected account's relevant posts during its inspection. Complete the monthly pass across the selected set before optional broader investigations of promising replies, linked products, and newly discovered people. Referenced posts from unselected categories or other people may be read as supporting evidence; this does not authorize a full scan of those categories. Keep the path from the watchlist account to each additional lead. New people are supplemental leads, not silently approved watchlist additions. Follow-up requests can extend the time window, add categories, or investigate selected threads or opportunities more deeply without redoing completed coverage.

## Expand promising signals

Use searches suited to the evidence already found:

- Requests and dissatisfaction: phrases such as "wish there was", "someone should build", "why is there no", or a specific expensive/manual task.
- Timely conversations: new capabilities, launches, changing workflows, confusing choices, or a topic that generates many concrete questions.
- Workarounds: people maintaining spreadsheets, repeatedly copying information, collecting links, or doing a task by hand.
- Shareable experiences: people comparing results, displaying an identity or achievement, inviting others, or circulating a useful resource.
- Distribution openings: a specific request or discussion where a small working product would make a relevant contribution.
- Proven businesses: launches, paying customers, repeat use, and founder results that suggest demand for another similar product or a close functional replica. Inspect how customers were acquired and what they bought; a new feature or an unserved niche is not required.

Use the required monthly window for the account pass and extend beyond it when a source chain or the user's follow-up calls for older context. Inspect both recent posts and stronger engagement where useful. Read replies to the most promising signals; search for independent accounts with the same need. Reposts and repeated promotional text do not constitute independent demand.

Follow visible browser state and the available tool documentation. Adapt queries to what the page actually supports. Avoid blind query grids, exhaustive historical timeline collection, or treating suggested search phrases as a required sequence. If an extra search family is unproductive, move on. The mandatory account pass is complete only when every selected account has a recorded inspection or access outcome; finding a few good leads is not a stopping condition. End supplemental exploration when the findings can be reported usefully and further searching is unlikely to resolve a material uncertainty.

## Record what was actually observed

For promising evidence capture:

- Direct post URL, author/account, post date, and observation date (with timezone when time-sensitive).
- A short paraphrase of the request or behavior; distinguish the author's assertion from independent verification.
- Visible replies, likes, reposts, views, or bookmarks where useful. Label missing counts as unavailable. Counts are a snapshot, not an estimate of future performance.
- Relevant reply themes, independent corroboration, workarounds, explicit purchase intent, and counterevidence. Note when replies or the full post could not be read.
- Whether the post is promotional, sponsored, a launch announcement, or an actual request; whether the proposed solution already exists, and what evidence its customers, usage, or purchases provide. Existing solutions do not make a relevant signal a "Nothing relevant found" result.

Search snippets are leads. Open the original evidence when possible before relying on it. Never manufacture posts, counts, customer quotes, or access. One popular post is a lead; do not call it a sustained trend without corroboration over time or multiple independent signals. A low-engagement but concrete paid problem can be stronger than a large vague conversation.

## Present all findings and an overall top five

Deliver all relevant findings in chat, grouped by selected category and account, with direct evidence links and concise explanations. State the selected categories, total unique accounts, and date window. Deduplicate repeated claims without losing their contributing accounts. Include additional leads with their discovery path and a complete coverage table for the selected accounts. Say "Nothing relevant found" for each fully inspected account without relevant signals; distinguish incomplete coverage and blocked results. Do not hide the full findings in a saved file or limit the full report to five ideas. Use numbered message parts when needed.

After the full report, add a ranked **top five opportunities overall for this run**, drawn from all selected accounts and their supplemental leads. Rank distinct opportunities rather than posts, follower counts, or category representatives. Use the comparison criteria below and explain the evidence and main tradeoff behind each rank. Include the target user and problem, supporting links, smallest useful product, sharing or first-user path, payer and purchase with a price hypothesis, rough effort/cost, main uncertainty, and next validation step. Label hypotheses and evidence gaps. If fewer than five opportunities have credible support, report fewer and explain the shortfall.

Then wait for the user's direction. They may ask to filter further, compare, extend the time window, investigate reference chains, or develop ideas. For deeper ideation, examine several plausible directions from the collected signals and distinguish proposed products from observed demand. Follow-up analysis uses the current pass rather than restarting it.

## Compare opportunities for the top five and follow-ups

Use qualitative judgments (strong, mixed, weak, or unknown) with short reasons. Avoid numerical scores that imply more certainty than the evidence supports. Compare:

| Dimension | What matters |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | A specific user and task, frequency or intensity of need, independent signals, existing workarounds, willingness to pay |
| Timing | Why this is relevant now, launch window, evidence of persistence or rapid decay |
| Sharing | A concrete output/action worth sharing, the sharer's motivation, recipient value, path to more users |
| Distribution | Reachable conversations and communities, first-user plan, dependence on a single person's response |
| Buildability | Small complete user journey, data availability, integrations, external permissions, ongoing support and maintenance |
| Economics | Who pays and why, suitable pricing model, likely variable costs, launch costs, payment friction |
| Competition and market entry | Existing alternatives, reachable customers, core functionality users expect, acquisition routes, switching friction where relevant, and room for a viable small business; feature novelty is optional |

Prioritize sharing, reachable users, and fast testing for this founder, while checking whether the money mechanism is plausible. Similar products and replicas of established functionality can be strong opportunities. Do not require a unique feature, a niche that nobody serves, or a durable moat, and do not rank a novel idea above a proven category solely because it is original. Price, language, service, convenience, positioning, or a distribution channel can help, but none is a mandatory invented differentiator. A competent alternative for the same audience can qualify if there is a credible way to reach and serve enough customers. Avoid equating complex infrastructure or expensive data access with a competitive advantage by itself.

Check the most relevant alternatives using current official pages, pricing, and docs to understand proven functionality, pricing, customer expectations, and operating costs. Describe the proposed audience and a practical route to a small share of its demand; the audience may be the same as a competitor's. Distinguish new buyers from customers who would have to switch, and assess switching friction only where it applies. Do not force an unmet gap, claim there is no competition, or treat a crowded market as an automatic rejection. Competition can support demand while also making acquisition harder; investigate both. A large market alone does not establish achievable sales. Confirm material API/data dependencies before recommending a build; label unverified costs and access requirements. Do not assume browser-readable X content creates a free production API for a proposed product.

For every shortlisted idea, specify who pays, what they receive, a tentative price or pricing basis, the likely route to the first payment, and the costs that affect profit. Do not count "ads later" or "sponsorship" as a complete earning method: state the audience or traffic prerequisite and how advertisers or sponsors would be reached. Acknowledge when the earning method is weak instead of using virality to hide it.

For a playful experience, explain why anyone pays, sponsors, or returns after sharing. For a utility, explain how sharing happens naturally without exposing private user information. For directories and marketplaces, account for sourcing data and acquiring the first participants; a simple interface does not remove the cold-start problem. Fit the first usable release into a week; reduce scope or explain why a candidate misses the founder's launch target.

## Turn evidence into a test

For the recommended idea, specify:

- One target user, one promise, and the smallest complete experience; list the main features to leave out.
- A plausible sharing loop: trigger → shareable artifact/action → recipient value → product visit → further participation.
- A concrete route to the first users, including a relevant X thread if observed and a fallback independent of an influencer responding.
- A revenue hypothesis appropriate to the product: one-time purchase, subscription, sponsorship, paid placement, or another explicit model. Do not force recurring billing on one-use value.
- Transparent revenue arithmetic toward at least USD 5,000 cumulative revenue over six months, including payer count and price. For example, 100 purchases at USD 50 produces USD 5,000 gross revenue before costs and fees; that is arithmetic, not a demand forecast. Recurring-revenue scenarios must account for when customers join and how long they stay. Show estimated costs and possible profit separately, stating which costs or fees remain unknown.
- Rough effort, launch cost, recurring cost, usage costs, operational burden, and important unknowns. Distinguish revenue from profit.
- The cheapest next test, the user's outreach tasks, a suitable observation window, and continue/change/stop thresholds tied to meaningful behavior such as activation, sharing, return use, or payment. Choose thresholds per idea rather than universal quotas.

Keep facts, interpretation, and proposed tests distinct. If evidence is thin, recommend a reversible test or a watch status rather than a confident build recommendation.
