## P013 Route briefings

**Target user and promise.** An independent driving instructor helping a learner or returning driver prepare for a particular journey. A mobile web link explains three to five difficult points before departure. The route and teaching notes come from the instructor. The hypothesis is that learners benefit from a short, accessible briefing without adopting another full lesson-management app.

**Evidence versus inference.** E01–E03 support stressful-route demand. They do not establish that route preparation is an adequate substitute for choosing a calmer route, or that instructors will pay. A separate [Waze community request from August 2, 2026](https://support.google.com/waze/thread/456805043/driving-backroads-instead-of-4-lane-highways?hl=en) describes unwanted detours onto smaller roads, providing evidence outside the viral thread that fastest-route preferences can conflict with driver preferences. Its community reply is not authoritative product documentation.

**Competition.** [Waze's official help](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/7666303?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) documents an option to avoid difficult intersections. [DriveNotes](https://www.drivenotes.co.uk/) advertises shared GPS routes, a library of tricky locations with learner hints, and a student app, at £9.99/month or £99/year. Its separate [about page](https://about.drivenotes.co.uk/) describes early access and optional route add-ons, inconsistent with the main page's packaging. Treat both as vendor claims; availability was not tested. The overlap is substantial. A simple share link is only a proposed distinction, and the full incumbent may be better value.

**Smallest complete product.** Instructor creates a reusable briefing, adds ordered location links and their own short notes, previews the mobile page, and shares a revocable private link. Learner reads it and marks points to discuss. Copying a briefing for another learner provides repeat value. No automatic safety rating, route optimization, live navigation, GPS recording, AI driving advice, or school-management suite in the first version. Instructor-authored content and a last-reviewed date are part of the product, because conditions and individual abilities differ.

**Data and build feasibility.** A text-and-links pilot needs no road-data ingestion. [Google Maps URLs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started) can open location and Street View views without an API key. That does not provide a free routing dataset, image reuse rights, guaranteed imagery coverage, or a promise that navigation will follow the instructor's exact route. Keep locations as distinct external links. Estimated founder effort with agent assistance: 24–30 hours, comprising 4 hours of workflow/content design, 10–12 of authoring and sharing, 4 of privacy and link controls, 4–6 of mobile verification, and 2–4 of deployment/setup. This estimate assumes the manual test first establishes a use case and that billing can initially be handled manually through an already available payment method.

**Sharing and first users.** Instructor sends the briefing to a learner; the learner can share it with their supervising family member. The recipient sees practical trip preparation and may ask their own instructor for it. This is a small professional referral loop, not evidence of viral growth. E01 is a place to discuss the underlying need if the user chooses to post, but its author responding is not the acquisition plan. The dependable route is five independent instructors in one city who currently use messages, notes, or PDFs. Country and city have not been selected; the user should choose a market where they can actually reach instructors. Do not combine left-driving and right-driving instructional content.

**Earning hypothesis.** Instructors buy reusable briefing creation and private sharing for **USD 59 per year**. This is a test price, not observed willingness to pay. Offer a **USD 25 assisted pilot**, credited toward the annual purchase; do not double count the credit as revenue. An instructor using an adequate existing app has little reason to add this expense.

**Six-month arithmetic.** 85 annual purchases × USD 59 = **USD 5,015 collected** within six months. That requires about 14–15 new paying instructors a month, before allowing for refunds. With a hypothetical 10% conversion from qualified trials, it needs about 850 trials. There is no evidence of that reach or conversion. Annual cash collected includes a year of service obligations and is not the same as six months of earned subscription revenue. Recognizing six months of service at most would require at least 170 customers paying from day one; staggered arrivals require more. The USD 5,000 goal therefore remains unvalidated even when the cash scenario works.

Budget assumptions: USD 30–100 setup, USD 10–30/month hosting and storage at pilot scale, 4% of receipts reserved for payment fees/refunds, and USD 200–500 for support and miscellaneous cash costs. On USD 5,015 receipts, these imply roughly USD 4,034–4,524 cash surplus before founder labor, taxes, and the remaining annual service obligation. Payment eligibility and actual fees depend on the user's merchant setup and market. No paid data or generation API is required for the narrow version. Initial acquisition and ongoing support could make the effective hourly return unattractive.

**Cheapest next test.** Spend 2–4 hours preparing one example from an instructor's own material using existing document tools. The user personally approaches five instructors, asks how they currently prepare learners for tricky locations, and shows the same example alongside DriveNotes and ordinary messaging. Do not pitch an unbuilt navigation engine. Run a seven-day pilot only if instructors recognize a gap.

- **Continue investigating:** at least two instructors pay USD 25, each sends a briefing to a real learner, at least three learners review it before their planned journey, and at least one instructor requests a second briefing. Record whether it actually reduces explanation or preparation effort.
- **Change:** learners use the briefings, but instructors will only pay for done-for-you preparation. Test a priced service and account for preparation time before building software.
- **Stop:** no paid pilot after five substantive conversations, the incumbent or a PDF meets the need, or users primarily need automatic route selection. That last need is outside the one-week scope.

These are deliberately small screening thresholds, not market validation. No outreach or pilot was conducted in this run.

## P014 Saved posts applied to a project

**Target and product.** A developer saves a practical coding technique but never adapts it to an existing project. Start with one supported stack and a user-pasted post plus a public sample project. Return a task brief containing source attribution, relevant files, acceptance checks, and instructions for the user's existing coding agent. The first manual experiment should deliver an actual reviewed small change so it tests useful implementation, not whether a summary looks impressive.

E04 provides an explicit first-user opening; E05 supports the wider unused-bookmark behavior. [Socialmemory](https://socialmemory.dev/) already brings X likes/bookmarks into coding agents for USD 12/month. [Dewey](https://getdewey.co/pricing/) offers bookmark organization, exports, and AI features, with Pro listed at USD 10/month or USD 7.50/month on annual billing. Dotmark search-indexed material described action plans, but its page returned HTTP 503 twice, so its current live functionality is unverified. A general bookmark organizer is a poor entry point. Directly pasting a post into an existing coding agent is the most important free substitute.

The potential distinction is completing a suitable change with clear verification. That is hard to deliver consistently across arbitrary projects. A narrow web form and brief export could fit 20–28 hours with agent assistance; reliable autonomous implementation and full X syncing do not fit this estimate. No X API access or paid data permission has been verified. Use pasted content in the test. Estimated setup USD 20–100, recurring infrastructure USD 10–30/month, and an assumed USD 0.20–2 model budget per brief if generation is hosted; actual model costs and user demand would need measurement. Running the actual coding agent remains the user's cost.

**Sharing:** builders voluntarily share a public before/after change with a link to the original technique and the tool; other builders try the same technique. Private code must not become a public artifact by default. **Earning:** test USD 19 for a manually assisted first change; a possible product price is USD 49 for a capped pack of ten project briefs. 103 packs × USD 49 = USD 5,047 gross over six months, but brief generation alone has weak differentiation. At the upper assumed model cost, ten uses cost USD 20 per pack before support, leaving materially less surplus. Do not price unlimited execution or founder review into this package.

**Next test:** the user can approach the requester and four other developers, using their own posting/conversations. Compare the proposed result with ordinary paste-and-prompt on a small real task. Require two USD 19 purchases and repeat use by at least two testers within a week before further investigation. Stop if existing agents deliver an equivalent result with negligible additional effort. Demand confidence: low; buildability of the limited brief tool: reasonable; confidence in a profitable distinction: low.

## P015 AI usage explained by task

**Target and product.** Frequent AI subscription users want to understand which work consumed their allowance. A limited web ledger could record task labels and user-provided before/after usage readings, show changes, and compare weeks. This is a measurement aid, not a guaranteed conversion between percentages and tokens.

E06 contains two accounts describing Grok Bot frustration; E07 is a separate cross-provider complaint with a personal workaround. [Official usage documentation](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/usage-limits) already describes a shared weekly pool, a live usage view and reset date, and differing usage rates by product. [Grok Bot's FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq) also explains plan-dependent usage. Those pages do not establish a supported public per-task subscription telemetry API for this proposed product. Developer API usage is not automatically subscription usage.

**Small version and cost:** a manual ledger with local storage, import/export, and charts could fit 12–18 hours, USD 0–50 setup and USD 0–15/month hosting. A reliable automatic cross-provider tracker cannot be estimated until supported data access is established. Manual entry may be too burdensome, and delayed counters or concurrent tasks make before/after attribution unreliable.

**Sharing:** users voluntarily share a redacted usage comparison showing model/settings and task type, linking to an empty template others can use. **Earning:** hypothesize a USD 19 one-time personal ledger license. 264 purchases × USD 19 = USD 5,016 gross; this requires substantial distribution for a product competing with free dashboards and spreadsheets. No such traffic or willingness to pay was observed. Hosted long-term history would add ongoing costs despite one-time billing.

**Next test:** ask five affected users to record five tasks each using their current official dashboard. Continue only if at least three can collect useful comparable readings, two return after a week, and two will buy at USD 19. Stop the automatic version if supported telemetry is unavailable, and stop the manual version if recording effort prevents repeat use. Demand for transparency: moderate confidence. Demand for this particular tool and feasibility of automatic collection: low confidence.

### P019 — PDF Listing Pack

**Buyer and problem.** An independent seller who already makes planners, workbooks, worksheets or printable guides repeatedly prepares promotional images and a preview for each finished product. Start with multi-page printable sellers on Etsy. TPT can be a later preset, not a second launch market requiring separate research.

**Concrete example.** A seller drops in their 30-page meal-planning PDF, chooses six pages to show and enters a title. They receive a coordinated cover image, a page-spread collage, two detail images, a “what is included” card, a download-instructions card and a separate watermarked sample PDF. They review every image before payment, pay to unlock the clean marketing pack, then upload it themselves. The preview watermark is seller-selected and does not imply protection against copying.

**Smallest useful product.** One PDF, maximum 50 pages/25 MB, six static listing images, three original visual styles, one editable title/subtitle and page selection. Keep the actual source page pixels; never redraw product text with image generation. Let the seller confirm page counts, sizes and inclusions. Do not automatically claim editability, commercial rights or learning outcomes. Explain unsupported or password-protected files before checkout. Export locally where practical and avoid retaining the original document. No Etsy login, automatic listing publishing, general design editor, customer-specific template creation, AI artwork production or guaranteed SEO ranking in version one.

**What AI does.** AI can help implement PDF rendering, layout, export, purchase entitlement and the website, and help create original template designs and marketing examples. Runtime AI is optional for short editable copy suggestions. The valuable result is a finished asset pack, not a generated paragraph. Ordinary orders must not require the founder to review or fix the design.

**Competition and proposed distinction.** [EasyList](https://easylistfordesigners.com/) offers cut-file sellers ten listing images, five free listings and Pro from $19/month. [Custom Mockup Generator](https://custommockupgenerator.com/) already produces complete branded listing sets, so a generic “all your listing images” claim is not differentiated. [ResizArt](https://resizart.com/) sells low-cost wall-art export credits, making a simple art resizer unattractive. P019's proposed distinction is selecting real pages from one multi-page PDF and producing both the promotional set and a controlled sample PDF in one short flow. Whether this saves enough effort over existing tools is still unknown. Do not claim that no tool supports it.

**Price hypothesis.** One useful preview image free; $9 for one complete listing pack or $29 for credits covering 20 products. Credits attach to a product, with re-downloads and a bounded revision window, rather than charging for every failed export. Both prices need testing; no lifetime hosting or unlimited manual service is included. Sellers, not their customers, pay.

**Build estimate.** Approximately 26–30 founder hours plus agent assistance: 4 hours for original templates and example assets, 8 for PDF/page selection and layouts, 5 for exports, 5 for checkout/credit recovery/deployment, and 4–8 for usability, file limits and browser QA. This is a thin desktop-first web release, not a full Canva replacement. Payment-provider availability, account verification and permitted use of fonts/assets are prerequisites; no account setup or purchase has been performed. Estimated cash setup $20–100 and infrastructure $15–40/month at small scale. These are planning allowances, not vendor quotes. Target variable processing below $0.05 per completed product by keeping image work in the browser; measure this before offering large credit packs.

**Marketing and sharing.** Publish short before/after demonstrations: “This PDF became these six listing images.” Use owned demo documents. Initial channels are YouTube tutorials for printable sellers, Pinterest examples and public posts in communities that allow promotion. Search pages should answer specific tasks such as making planner listing images or creating a printable-product preview. The recipient of a sample PDF is generally a buyer, not another seller, so there is no assumed viral loop. A seller may share the workflow with other sellers; useful demo content and search are the primary acquisition mechanisms.

**Founder work.** Make two or three demonstrations per week, improve high-traffic examples, inspect anonymous completion/purchase metrics, and handle a small support inbox. No DMs, customer interviews or calls are required for the proposed test. Avoid custom layout offers: those would turn the product back into client work.

**No-call validation.** After selection, first spend 6–10 hours on a single working style with a real sample output and a free export. Advertise the proposed paid options truthfully; if checkout is not yet implemented, label the button as early-access interest and do not count clicks as sales. Aim for 200 relevant visits from public demonstrations and permitted posts over 14 days. Continue to the paid release if at least 20 visitors load their own valid PDF, at least 12 finish a usable preview, and at least 8 select a paid option. Those are chosen decision thresholds, not benchmark conversion rates. If fewer than 200 relevant visits arrive, distribution remains untested rather than the product disproven.

For the working paid release, require at least five purchases per 200 relevant visits, fewer than one in ten completed packs needing founder help, and at least three paying users producing a second product within 30 days. If people finish previews but do not buy, change the offer or price once. If inputs regularly require individual repair, shrink the supported format. Stop after a second adequately trafficked test if fewer than two buyers purchase or the output is interchangeable with a free workflow. Do not use manual fulfillment to make the metrics look successful.

**Six-month revenue scenario.** Selling 173 of the $29 credit packs produces $5,017 gross revenue. At an assumed 2% visitor-to-purchase rate, that needs 8,650 qualified visits, about 1,442 per month. At 1%, traffic needs double. Assume all credits are redeemed: 173 × 20 = 3,460 processed products. This is an arithmetic scenario, not a forecast or observed conversion rate.

Illustrative six-month deductions: $387 payment/platform fees using a conservative placeholder of 6% plus $0.50/order, $251 refund reserve (5%), $180 hosting, $173 processing at the target $0.05/product, $100 setup/assets and $600 optional marketing. Total about $1,691; possible operating surplus about $3,326 before founder labor and income tax. Actual provider fees and regional tax handling require checking when choosing checkout. At $10 acquisition cost per purchase, marketing alone would be $1,730 and the surplus falls by $1,130. The unproven part is reaching relevant sellers cheaply, not generating a PDF. There has been no spend or payment collection.

**Confidence:** high fit with the requested self-serve operating model; reasonable confidence in the narrow technical scope; low confidence in paid demand and the proposed differentiation. Best next action is the small test, not an elaborate platform build.

### P018 — App Launch Image Kit

**Buyer and result.** A solo developer preparing a first mobile-app release uploads six real screenshots and an icon, chooses one of three styles, edits suggested benefit headlines and downloads matching store screenshots plus square/portrait social launch images. Start with English and one mobile platform. No account connection, store submission, ad management, video rendering or promised approval. This is a familiar, visually demonstrable purchase with no sales call.

E10 is direct demand for the task. E11 is a recent counterexample: a developer claims an existing coding agent already does it. [AppScreens](https://appscreens.com/pricing) has a free plan with five screens and Pro at $99/year billed annually. More damaging to the proposed one-time positioning, [Screenhance](https://screenhance.com/) already offers a coordinated multi-surface image workflow and a $6 one-time Launch Pass for ten exports over seven days. [FrameFlux](https://www.frameflux.app/pricing) lists Creator at $7.99/month. One-time billing and a complete bundle are therefore **not established differentiators**.

**Testable offer and effort.** Hypothesize $19 per app launch with limited regenerations. A three-style static-image product could fit 22–30 hours with agent assistance, including checkout, current store-size checks, export QA and deployment. Budget $20–100 setup and $15–40/month infrastructure, plus metered copy generation if enabled. Reliable video and multilingual proofreading are excluded. Store dimensions and requirements must be verified against official store documentation at implementation time.

**Distribution and sharing.** Public before/after videos on X and YouTube, a free launch image, and tutorials for first-time app builders. Buyers can share a branded free output voluntarily; paid outputs are clean. The proposed promise is fewer design decisions, not higher app downloads. The user personally publishes demonstrations and handles occasional support.

**Next test.** Use the same six owned app screenshots to make the proposed pack and a pack with an existing tool. Record completion time and compare actual outputs. Proceed only if a public self-serve demo generates five $19 purchases per 200 relevant visits and most buyers finish without help. Stop if the output or completion time offers no clear improvement over the $6 alternative. At $19, 264 purchases produce $5,016 gross over six months, before fees, refunds, acquisition and hosting. Price pressure makes this a weaker first test than P019. Demand for the general task is credible; demand for another supplier remains unproven.

### Other directions checked in the revision

- **PDF-to-EPUB repair:** E16 and other search results show reader frustration. But [Leafbind](https://leafbind.io/pricing) offers free/basic conversion and small paid credit packs, while users recommend free alternatives. Complex conversion quality and per-document support work conflict with the preferred operating model. Not shortlisted.

### Scope and outcome of the revision

Research broadened from recent X posts to older explicit task requests where recent results were noisy. Many posts were promotions, automated launch announcements or long unrelated articles and were excluded as demand evidence. Official competitor pages were read, but their applications were not purchased or fully tested. Seller prices and testimonials are not audited revenue. The proposed P019 workflow is an inference from adjacent X friction, a directly offered paid template workflow and marketplace preview support; no direct current X request for this exact bundle was established.

No outreach, external posting, subscriptions, accounts, payments or product implementation occurred. The original report and decisions are preserved above. The user's marketing-led, minimal-client-contact constraint applies to this investigation, and IDEA_DIRECTIONS.md remains untouched.
